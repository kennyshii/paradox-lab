01 The paradox

Before you can walk across a room, you have to reach the halfway point. Before you can cover the rest, you have to reach half of what remains: the three-quarter mark. Then seven-eighths, then fifteen-sixteenths, and so on without end. That is infinitely many tasks, and you can’t complete infinitely many tasks one after another. So, Zeno of Elea argued around 450 BC, you can never arrive. In fact, you can never even start. Yet people cross rooms all the time. What’s wrong with the argument?

02 Make a guess

Where does the argument go wrong?

Half plus a quarter plus an eighth, and so on forever, adds up to exactly one whole. The infinitely many stages fit inside a normal distance and a normal amount of time, because each one takes half as long as the one before.

04 Why it works

  • 🚶

    Shrinking steps

    Each half-step is shorter than the last, and takes less time too. So the steps get smaller and quicker forever.

  • 🟦

    Infinitely many pieces, one total

    Lay the halves side by side and they fill exactly one whole: ½ plus ¼ plus ⅛ and so on never goes past 1.

  • ⏱️

    You arrive on time

    Walking at a steady pace, you reach the door when you expect. The “infinitely many steps” are just a way of slicing up an ordinary walk.
