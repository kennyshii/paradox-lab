01 The paradox

You’re on a game show facing three closed doors. Behind one is a car; behind the other two, goats. You pick a door. The host, who knows where the car is, then opens one of the other doors to reveal a goat, and offers you a choice: stick with your original door, or switch to the one remaining closed door. Two doors are left, one car. Surely it’s a coin flip, so does switching make any difference?

02 Make a guess

What should you do?

Switching wins 2/3 of the time; staying wins only 1/3. Try it below, then run a few thousand simulated rounds.

04 Why it works

  • 🚪

    Your first pick is stuck at 1 in 3

    When you choose, you have a 1 in 3 chance. The host opening a goat door doesn’t change that, because he can always find a goat to show you.

  • ➡️

    The rest moves to the other door

    The other two doors held a 2 in 3 chance together. Once one is shown to be a goat, that whole chance sits on the door he left closed.

  • 💯

    Picture 100 doors

    Pick 1 of 100, and the host opens 98 goats, skipping just one door. That door he avoided is almost certainly the car. Three doors work the same way, just less dramatically.
