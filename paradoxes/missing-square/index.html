01 The paradox

Four pieces (two triangles and two L-shapes) fit together into a right triangle 13 squares wide and 5 tall. Slide the same four pieces into a new arrangement, without rotating or resizing anything, and you get the same 13 × 5 triangle again… except now there is a one-square hole in it. The pieces haven’t changed, so their total area can’t have changed. Where did the extra square come from?

02 Make a guess

What’s really going on?

The two triangles have different slopes (3/8 vs 2/5), so the “hypotenuse” bends: inward in the first figure, outward in the second. The difference is exactly one square.

04 Why it works

Two slopes that look the same

The “hypotenuse” is made of the long edges of the two triangles. Their slopes are

$$\text{large triangle: } \frac{3}{8} = 0.375, \qquad \text{small triangle: } \frac{2}{5} = 0.4, \qquad \text{true diagonal: } \frac{5}{13} \approx 0.385.$$

Because the slopes differ, the two edges don’t line up into one straight line. In the first arrangement the shallow triangle comes first, so the edge sags below the true diagonal. In the second the steep one comes first, so it bulges above it. The largest deviation is only \(1/13\) of a square, which is invisible at a glance.

Counting the area

The pieces have total area

$$\underbrace{12}_{8\times 3/2} + \underbrace{5}_{5\times2/2} + 8 + 7 = 32.$$

A genuine 13 × 5 triangle would have area \(13 \times 5 / 2 = 32.5\). So:

  • Arrangement 1 is a slightly concave shape of area \(32 = 32.5 - 0.5\).
  • Arrangement 2 is a slightly convex shape of area \(33 = 32.5 + 0.5\), which is 32 of pieces plus the 1-square hole.

Each sliver between the bent edge and the true diagonal is a thin triangle; its area comes from the cross-product of its sides:

$$\tfrac12\,\lvert 8\cdot 5 - 3\cdot 13\rvert = \tfrac12, \qquad \tfrac12\,\lvert 5\cdot 5 - 2\cdot 13\rvert = \tfrac12.$$

The two half-square slivers add up to exactly the missing square.

Why it’s falsidical

The argument “same pieces, same triangle, so same area” has a false premise hidden in plain sight: the figures are not triangles. The puzzle works because 2, 3, 5, 8 and 13 are consecutive Fibonacci numbers, whose ratios are all close to each other, so the bend is tiny.
