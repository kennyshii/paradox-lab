01 The paradox

A year has 365 days. Gather people in a room one by one: how many do you need before there’s a better-than-even chance that some two of them share a birthday? With 365 possible days, it feels like you’d need a crowd in the hundreds, or at least half of 365. The real answer is small enough that most school classes have a shared birthday hiding in them.

02 Make a guess

How many people for a 50% chance of a shared birthday?

Just 23 people give a 50.7% chance. With 70 people it’s 99.9%.

04 Why it works

  • 🎂

    It’s not about your birthday

    Matching your own birthday is rare. But the question asks about any two people, and that’s a very different number of chances.

  • 🤝

    Count the pairs

    23 people form 253 different pairs. Each pair is a small chance of a shared birthday, and 253 small chances add up to just over 50%.

  • 📈

    Pairs grow fast

    Double the people and the pairs roughly quadruple. That’s why 70 people make a match almost certain.
