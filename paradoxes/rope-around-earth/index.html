01 The paradox

Wrap a rope snugly around the Earth’s equator, about 40,000 km of it. Now cut it and splice in just one extra metre, then lift the rope evenly all the way round so it hovers above the ground. One metre spread over forty million metres sounds like nothing. How big is the gap: could an ant crawl under it, or a cat? And if you instead grabbed the rope at one point and pulled it straight up until it went taut, how high could you lift it?

02 Make a guess

Lifted evenly, the rope with 1 extra metre floats about…

The gap is about 16 cm (one metre divided by roughly six), and it is the same for a basketball, the Earth or the Sun. Pulled up at a single point, the same metre lifts the Earth rope about 121 m.

04 Why it works

  • 🌍

    A circle’s length follows its size

    Make a circle a little wider and its length grows by about six times as much. That’s true for every circle, tiny or huge.

  • ✂️

    One metre buys a sixth of a metre

    Lifting the rope by some amount needs about six times that much extra rope. So one extra metre lifts it about 16 cm.

  • 🤔

    Why it feels wrong

    We compare one metre with 40,000 km and decide it’s nothing. But the gap only depends on the extra rope, not on how big the planet is.
