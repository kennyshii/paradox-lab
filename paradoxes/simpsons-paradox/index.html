01 The paradox

A 1986 study compared two treatments for kidney stones. For small stones, treatment A had the higher success rate. For large stones, treatment A had the higher success rate again. Yet when the two groups were combined, treatment B came out ahead overall. How can something be better in every group and worse in total? Surely an average of “better” and “better” is “better”?

02 Make a guess

What explains the reversal?

Doctors gave treatment A mostly to the hard cases (large stones). A’s overall rate is dragged down by its difficult workload, not by being worse.

    04 Why it works

    • 🧪

      Better in every group

      Treatment A really did better for small stones and for large stones. Nothing about that is fake.

    • ⚖️

      But the groups were lopsided

      A was mostly given the hard cases. B was mostly given easy ones. Totals mix those very different situations together.

    • 🔍

      Compare like with like

      When the mix of cases differs, the overall total can point the wrong way. Always ask how each group was made up.
